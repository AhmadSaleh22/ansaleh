---
title: Watch them work before you ask
date: 2026-07-12
summary: Interviews tell you what people think they do. Watching tells you what they actually do.
---

Most requirements arrive as a sentence: "operators need a faster way to plan," "finance needs better reports." The sentence is true and almost useless. It tells you where the pain is, not what causes it.

When I started on the planning platform at Syntax, I asked operators how they plan a production run. Their answers were tidy and short. Then I watched them do it. A real process almost always has steps nobody mentions: a workaround, a number checked somewhere else, a value copied by hand from one screen to another. They don’t come up in interviews because, to the person doing them, they aren’t "planning." They’re just how the day goes.

## What I do now

I interview first, to learn the words people use and what they think matters. Then I watch at least one real session from start to finish without helping. I write down every step, every switch between tools and every time someone waits.

That list becomes a journey map the whole team can read. It turns a vague request into a set of specific steps, and it makes prioritizing easy: you rebuild the steps that cost the most time or cause the most mistakes first.

## Why it matters for engineering

Observation changes technical decisions, not just design ones. At Syntax it is why planning starts from a recommended plan instead of a blank screen. At Eddekhar, the conversations with finance and HR shaped a data model built around how employees, companies and payroll records relate to each other.

### The cost

It takes days, and it can feel slow when a team wants to start building. Building the wrong thing quickly is slower.
